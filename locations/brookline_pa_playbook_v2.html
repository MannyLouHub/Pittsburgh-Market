Underwriting Screen — Where the Deal Math Still Works (secondary)
MODEL BASIS
Screening cases assume 20% down, 7.75% interest, 10% PM of EGI, 8% vacancy, 10% repairs, 3% other expenses, $1,500 annual insurance, CLR-modeled taxes (50.14% × 27.307 mills). Duplex GPR $2,100/mo (2 × $1,050). Triplex GPR $2,975/mo (2 × $1,050 + 1 × $875).
TIER 1 — STRONG SCREEN (DUPLEX)
Under $155,000
🟢 DUPLEX MEETS ALL THRESHOLDS — MOVE QUICKLY
A duplex below $155K meets all benchmarks under the 27.307-mill structure. At $140K: ~$340/mo cash flow, 9.6% cap rate, and 1.43× DSCR. At $150K: ~$265/mo cash flow, 9.1% cap rate, and 1.33× DSCR. TIER 2 — WATCH THE MATH (DUPLEX)
$155,000 – $175,000
🟡 DUPLEX MARGINAL — VERIFY ALL INPUTS
At $165K, a duplex produces approximately $185/mo cash flow, 8.5% cap rate, and 1.24× DSCR — all benchmarks technically met but with thin margin. At $175K, CoC compresses toward the minimum and DSCR approaches 1.20×. In this range, each input (actual tax bills, executed rent comps, verified insurance) moves the needle materially — do not close without confirmed numbers.
TIER 3 — TRIPLEX OR QUAD REQUIRED
$175,000 – $225,000
🟡 DUPLEX WILL NOT PENCIL — 3+ UNITS REQUIRED
Above $175K, a duplex at conservative rents will fail one or more benchmarks even at 27.307 mills. A triplex at $185K ($2,975 GPR) produces ~$450/mo cash flow, 10.3% cap rate, and 1.54× DSCR — a strong model. In this tier, unit count becomes the acquisition filter. A 3–4 unit building at $200K still works; a duplex at $185K likely does not.
TIER 4 — PASS UNLESS INCOME IS DOCUMENTED
$225,000+
🔴 DUPLEX / TRIPLEX MARGINAL — QUAD WITH VERIFIED INCOME
Above $225K, even a triplex begins to strain at conservative rents. A quad at $225K ($4,200 GPR) produces approximately $700+/mo cash flow, 12%+ cap rate, and 1.7× DSCR — viable with confirmed 4 legal units. Do not model above $225K without documented in-place rent rolls and confirmed unit counts.

Property Management — Brookline / Southwest Pittsburgh Guide
✅ SELF-MANAGE: VIABLE
Brookline's low renter concentration (~30%) is an asset for self-managers — fewer lease events per year than majority-renter markets, and tenants here tend to stay longer. The city mill rate (27.31) means the PM fee doesn't compress margins the way it does in Brentwood. Self-managing is a real option for local southwest Pittsburgh investors. As with all city stock, have a reliable city-familiar contractor before you start — pre-1950 rowhouses generate maintenance surprises. Consider leasing-only PM for tenant placement; self-manage the ongoing relationship.
🏢 PM: GOOD OPTION IF NOT LOCAL
PM firms covering the south Pittsburgh corridor (Brookline, Beechview, Dormont) have solid coverage here. Full PM is the right answer if you're not local or scaling to multiple city properties. Brookline's recent rent growth (+16% YoY flag in the data) means re-lease timing matters — a PM with an active applicant pipeline can capture that growth faster than a self-manager who re-leases once a year.

THIN RENT COMP BASE — ASK PM FOR CURRENT 15226 DATA
With only ~30% renter-occupied stock and 78% single-family housing, Brookline PMs with genuine 15226-specific rental data are rare. When interviewing, press for their actual days-to-lease and current rent comps from within the ZIP — not aggregated Southwest Pittsburgh figures. A PM with no Brookline experience may price your unit incorrectly in either direction.
✅ HIGHER-INCOME TENANTS SUPPORT STRONG SCREENING
Median household income of $77,366 means Brookline renters generally qualify comfortably at $1,050/unit 2BR rents. A PM maintaining 3× income standards in this market has a larger qualifying tenant pool than in lower-income markets. Request screening standards upfront and ask about average placed-tenant credit scores.
