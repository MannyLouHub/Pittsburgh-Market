Property Management — Avalon / North Pittsburgh Guide
⚠️ SELF-MANAGE: VIABLE WITH CONDITIONS
Avalon's ~60% renter concentration means more frequent lease events than most playbook markets. Self-managing is viable if you're local to the north shore corridor and have real availability — but two conditions apply: (1) use a PM for tenant placement even if you self-manage day-to-day, since screening quality in this demographic profile matters significantly, and (2) if your properties contain HCV tenants, do not attempt to self-manage the ACHA process without prior experience. The ACHA inspection and HAP payment process requires familiarity that most first-time self-managers lack.
✅ PM: EARNS ITS FEE — ESPECIALLY FOR HCV
Avalon is where PM value is clearest. The combination of high renter concentration (frequent leasing activity) and significant HCV demand (ACHA payment standards above market rate) means a PM with active 15202 ACHA experience is delivering two things: faster leasing and higher rent. Ask every PM candidate: how many active HCV units do you currently manage in 15202, and what is your average ACHA inspection pass timeline? Those two answers tell you whether they can execute the strategy.

✅ HCV IS THE INCOME EDGE IN AVALON
ACHA 2BR payment standards for 15202 (~$1,050–$1,250/mo) exceed Avalon's market-rate average ($1,025) and the conservative underwrite ($900). A PM with established ACHA relationships in this corridor converts Avalon's lower-income demographic from a vacancy risk into a direct-deposit income stream. This is the single most important operational differentiator for Avalon investments.
BELLEVUE PM ≠ AVALON PM
Because 15202 covers both boroughs, some PMs claim Bellevue experience that doesn't translate to active Avalon units. Ask for Avalon-specific unit counts and tenant references. The two boroughs have different tax collectors, different code enforcement, and slightly different tenant profiles. A PM with deep Bellevue experience but minimal Avalon presence may underperform in ways that aren't visible until a unit turns over.
