Why Mt. Washington for Your Strategy
✅
CITY OF PITTSBURGH MILL RATE — LOW COMBINED STRUCTUREAt 26.35 combined mills, Mt. Washington shares the City of Pittsburgh's favorable tax structure with Brookline, Beechview, Brighton Heights, and Highland Park — roughly half the burden of Brentwood (56.63) and well below Dormont (40.84). A $180K duplex carries only ~$198/mo in estimated taxes — a durable margin advantage on back-street stock.
✅
WALK / INCLINE TO DOWNTOWN = STRUCTURAL RENTAL DEMANDMt. Washington is one of the only Pittsburgh neighborhoods where tenants can walk or ride an incline to the CBD. That proximity to downtown jobs, Station Square, and the hospital system underpins durable demand for well-run rentals — vacancy risk on a quality non-view unit is structurally low.
✅
BACK-STREET STOCK IS NORMAL CITY PRICINGAway from the Grandview/skyline blocks, Mt. Washington duplexes and triplexes price like the rest of southern City of Pittsburgh — $160K–$230K — and the right unit count pencils under conservative rents at the 26.35-mill structure: duplexes at the low end of the range, triplexes through the top. The buyable rental inventory is on these blocks, not on the view.
⚠️
THE VIEW PREMIUM IS A RENTAL TRAP — UNDERWRITE WITHOUT ITA downtown skyline view adds a large premium to purchase price on Grandview Ave and the upper blocks, but conservative long-term rent does not scale with the view the way price does. Never let a view justify a rental purchase — underwrite at non-view rents and non-view comps. See the Strategy tab: "The Mt. Washington Rule."
⚠️
HILLSIDE TOPOGRAPHY = REAL DILIGENCE COSTSteep streets, hillside foundations, retaining walls, city steps, and limited/permit parking are defining features of the bluff. These drive inspection scope, winter access cost, and insurance — budget for a hillside-experienced inspector and confirm retaining-wall condition before going to contract.
⚠️
BIMODAL MARKET — THE "MEDIAN" IS MISLEADINGThe blended ~$240K median mixes $400K+ view condos with sub-$200K back-street rowhouses. Do not anchor on neighborhood-wide medians; price each deal against comps from its own block type (view vs. non-view).

LOW CITY MILL RATE — MAJOR MARGIN ADVANTAGE ON BACK-STREET STOCK
At 26.35 combined mills, Mt. Washington carries the City of Pittsburgh's favorable structure — roughly half the rate of Brentwood (56.63) and meaningfully below Dormont (40.84) and Bellevue (42.18). A $180K back-street duplex pays only ~$198/mo in estimated property tax, leaving substantially more room for cash flow than the equivalent purchase in a high-mill borough. This advantage is real only if you buy at back-street pricing — a view-premium purchase erodes it instantly.

💡 TAX APPEAL — FILE AFTER CLOSE
Mt. Washington's older hillside stock and the wide gap between view and non-view values mean assessments can drift from a back-street property's actual market position. A BPAAR appeal targeting CLR-modeled assessed value (50.14% × purchase price) at 26.35 mills compounds positively even at the low city rate. File before September 1 each year for the following tax year, and document non-view comps to support a back-street appeal.

Build your comp support from the same block type before going to contract, and add a hillside-access contingency to the rehab budget for material staging and equipment.
THE MT. WASHINGTON RULE: NEVER PAY FOR A VIEW YOU CAN'T RENT
The skyline view is the most valuable — and most dangerous — feature in this ZIP. It adds a large premium to purchase price, but conservative long-term rent does not scale with it: a non-view 2BR underwrites at $1,100, and even a genuine view unit rarely supports the rent needed to justify view pricing on a buy-and-hold basis. If you are buying a view, buy it for appreciation or your own enjoyment (house hack) — not because the rental math works, because at view pricing it usually won't. Every rental deal here is underwritten at non-view rents against non-view comps.
SHORT-TERM RENTAL (STR) — OPTIONALITY, NOT A PRO-FORMA BASIS
Mt. Washington is one of the few Pittsburgh neighborhoods where a skyline view could support short-term rental demand. Treat this strictly as upside optionality, never as the basis for the purchase price. Confirm current City of Pittsburgh short-term-rental registration/zoning rules, condo or HOA restrictions, and insurance implications first — and require that the unit still cash-flows as a conventional long-term rental before you buy. A deal that only works as an STR is a speculation, not an investment in this playbook.

TIER 3 — TRIPLEX OR QUAD REQUIRED
$190,000 – $245,000
🟢 STRONG AS A TRIPLEX — DUPLEX WILL NOT PENCIL

In this tier, unit count is the acquisition filter: a 3–4 unit building works, a duplex at $230K does not.
TIER 4 — VIEW / OWNER-OCCUPANT TERRITORY
$245,000+
🔴 LIKELY VIEW-PREMIUM — NOT A CONVENTIONAL RENTAL
Above ~$245K even a triplex slips to marginal (at $255K: ~$314/mo, ~8.4% cap, ~5.6% CoC, ~1.21× DSCR), and pricing in 15211 this high usually carries a skyline-view premium that conservative rents cannot support. A quad with four documented legal units and verified in-place rents can still work; a duplex or triplex at this price almost never will as a rental. Treat this tier as house-hack, appreciation, or owner-occupant territory unless income is fully documented.

Property Management — Mt. Washington / South Pittsburgh Guide
✅ SELF-MANAGE: VIABLE — WITH A HILLSIDE CAVEAT
Mt. Washington's proximity to downtown makes it easy for a local owner to self-manage — you can be on-site in minutes via the bridge or incline. The 26.35-mill city rate means the PM fee doesn't compress margins the way it does in Brentwood. The real caveat is winter and topography: snow and ice on steep streets and city steps, hillside access for contractors, and retaining-wall upkeep are ongoing obligations. Self-managing is a genuine option for local owners with a reliable hillside-familiar contractor on call. Consider leasing-only PM for tenant placement and self-manage the relationship.
🏢 PM: GOOD OPTION IF NOT LOCAL OR HILLSIDE-AVERSE
Full PM is the right answer if you're not local, scaling across city properties, or unwilling to manage winter hillside access. PM firms covering southern Pittsburgh (Mt. Washington, Beechview, Brookline, the South Side) have solid coverage here. Mt. Washington's downtown-commuter renter pool re-leases quickly when priced correctly — a PM with an active applicant pipeline and genuine 15211 rent data can keep a non-view unit occupied with minimal downtime.

VIEW vs. NON-VIEW RENT DATA — PRESS THE PM FOR SPECIFICS
A PM quoting "Mt. Washington 2BR rents" may be blending view and non-view units into one misleading average. Press for executed, non-view comps from within 15211 for a property like yours. A PM who can't separate the two will likely mis-price your unit — high enough to sit vacant, or low enough to leave money on the table.
✅ DOWNTOWN-COMMUTER DEMAND SUPPORTS STRONG SCREENING
Mt. Washington's walk/incline access to downtown jobs attracts employed young professionals and service/hospitality workers. A PM maintaining 3× income standards has a steady qualifying pool here. Request screening standards upfront and ask about average placed-tenant credit scores and time-in-job.
